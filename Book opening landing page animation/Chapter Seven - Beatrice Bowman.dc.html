CHAPTER SEVEN
The Journey of Beatrice Robertson

Welcome to Chapter Seven. I am Cyrus Sylvester, and it is my absolute honor to invite you to step into the story of my dear aunt, Beatrice Robertson. Known affectionately to the entire family as “Aunt Bee,” she was the seventh child born into the loving marriage of Big Ma and Big Papa. Come along with me on this journey as we celebrate a woman who was a pillar in every measure of the word, and the standard in every sense of the word. While there is still more of our history to unfold in the chapters ahead, telling Aunt Bee’s story marks a beautiful milestone for our family: with her, we have now proudly accounted for all of the siblings. It was deeply important to us to ensure that each and every one of them was given something extra and special to honor their memory. This is her story.

Though she was one of the youngest among her siblings, Aunt Bee was the undeniable bedrock upon which this family was built in Pine Grove. Because of Beatrice’s constant, unwavering watchcare over Big Ma, her older siblings in New Orleans could rest easily at night. They had the ultimate assurance knowing that their little sister was providing their mother with the absolute pinnacle of attentiveness and genuine care in her later years.

Beatrice and her ever-present husband, D.J. Bowman, were blessed with the resources and the means to live anywhere of their choosing. Yet, they made the beautiful decision to call Pine Grove home. In doing so, they continued the Robertson traditions and customs, keeping the family legacy alive in the place we have always called home. To visit Pine Grove in the 70s and 80s was an experience unlike any other. You were instantly greeted by the sight of free-roaming cattle, the mouth-watering smell of goat roasting on the grill, and the taste of the sweetest watermelon God ever created, picked fresh from her garden on the forest side of the home.

Her Pride & Her Joy

Her greatest pride was her amazing marriage to Uncle D.J. Together, they built a home just a few paces away from the big house, choosing to stay wrapped in her mother’s love. To this union, eight wonderful children were born: three sons — Pete, Donald Wayne, and Torrence — and five daughters — Brenda, Patricia, Diane, Stacey, and Regina. Taking the baton of family continuity that Big Ma started, Aunt Bee raised some of the most generous and compassionate children our family has ever known.

Even with a bustling household, her heart was always connected to her sisters. Though they were a few hours away in New Orleans, Aunt Bee loved them fiercely and made sure they talked as often as the kids and daily chores would allow.

Her Legacy

When Big Ma passed away and the entire New Orleans branch of the family migrated back to the country to mourn, Aunt Bee immediately became the undeniable face of the family seat. Even in the midst of her own profound grief, she selflessly made our comfort her top priority. She became the very definition of hospitality, somehow finding a place for everyone to sleep and ensuring that every single mouth was fed. That was simply who she was — accommodating all and turning absolutely no one away. Because of her boundless grace, Aunt Bee became the reason we continued to make that journey down to Pine Grove long after Big Ma was gone.

Aunt Bee’s legacy is that of a true matriarch. She held the highest social and decision-making authority within her household, yet she beautifully shared that authority with her husband. Aunt Bee and Uncle D.J. displayed the absolute perfect family structure, serving as a shining example for anyone needing a blueprint on how to build and sustain a loving family.

While earlier records noted twenty-four grandchildren, her family tree has blossomed so vastly that there are now layers upon layers of grandchildren and great-grandchildren continuing her story. Her true legacy will forever live on in her warm, welcoming smile and her unparalleled devotion to her children, her husband, her siblings, and most of all, Big Ma.

On behalf of myself and all the generations that follow her, it is my absolute honor to formally reintroduce you to the bedrock of Pine Grove — our beloved Beatrice Robertson Bowman. Thank you so much for coming along with me on this journey to celebrate her beautiful, unforgettable life.
